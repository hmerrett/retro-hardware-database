{# A list of parts: the ones fitted in a machine, or the ones mounted on a card. One card each -- the tag and the kind on a strip across the top, the name under it, and what the part is made of beneath that. It began as a table, and a table is what a part is not. The specs are the longest thing about it and were a fourth column, so they took most of the width, squeezed the tag and the name into a few characters each, and still wrapped to three lines; moving them to a second row fixed the widths but left every part as five loose cells with a thin rule somewhere in the middle, and nothing on the page said which rule ended one part and began the next. A card says it: everything about one part is inside one border, and the strip at the top is where the eye lands when it is looking for a tag. The specs are labelled pairs in tracks rather than a run of "Key: value |", as many across as the card has room for. A part with nothing recorded gets no pairs at all, so the list also shows at a glance which parts have been written up and which are still just a name. Everywhere a part is listed uses this -- a machine's parts, a card's mounted parts, and the one motherboard above them -- so they cannot come to look like three different things. They differ only in what taking a part out is called: unlinked from a machine, unmounted from the card it sits on. `authed` is passed in because an imported macro does not see the request. #} {% macro cards(parts, endpoint, label, back, authed) %}
