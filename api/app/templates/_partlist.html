{# The things a page lists, drawn as cards: the parts fitted in a machine, the parts mounted on a card, the files kept beside the register, the chips in a machine's sockets. One card each -- what it is called on a strip across the top, and what it is underneath. They began as tables, and a table is what none of them is. The specs are the longest thing about a part and were a fourth column, so they took most of the width, squeezed the tag and the name into a few characters each, and still wrapped to three lines; putting them on a second row fixed the widths but left every part as five loose cells with a rule somewhere in the middle of it, and nothing on the page told you which rule ended one part and began the next. A card says it: everything about one thing is inside one border, and the strip at the top is where the eye lands when it is looking for a tag. One template for all of them, so a machine's parts, a card's mounted parts, the motherboard above them and the files beside them cannot come to look like four different things. Inside a panel the cards give up their own border and keep their bands, since the panel has already drawn the box (see _panel.html). #} {# Parts, each drawn by U.itemcard. `endpoint` is what taking one out does -- unlink it from a machine, detach it from the card it sits on -- and `authed` is passed in because an imported macro does not see the request. `thumbs` is a picture for each of them by asset id (see part_thumbs), and the way through to that part's own page, where its photographs are. A list of parts is otherwise the one place on this site that describes hardware without showing it: four Teac floppy drives read as four near-identical lines of text, and which is which is a thing you can see in a quarter of a second and cannot read at all. The drawing stands in where nobody has photographed it, as it does on the gallery card for the same part -- a card with no picture at all would read as a card still loading rather than as a thing nobody has photographed.
